# Operational acceptance policy

Declared on October 2, 2026 before the fresh final run. This policy covers the default Docker stack and a 900-second test with 10,000 devices, five-second intervals, 100 active zones and three subscribed sessions across two users. These are engineering budgets, not additional requirements from the assignment.

- Exactly 1,800,000 reports must be scheduled and accepted; every healthy session must reconcile its location count and report checksum. Both owner sessions must reconcile private alerts; the other user's private alert stream must remain empty.
- No generator drops, HTTP rejections, transport errors or failed reports.
- Accepted rate over the full elapsed time, including drain, must be at least 1,980 reports/s: 99% of the offered rate.
- Generator scheduling p99 must be at most 100 ms; at most 0.1% of reports may be more than 100 ms late, and maximum scheduling lag must be below one second. Late reports remain visible as warnings. The schedule still advances by fixed deadlines, never by HTTP completion.
- End-to-end delivery p95 must be below one second, measured from the scheduled report timestamp so producer and server queue delays remain included.
- Pending reports stay within 4,096, checked-out SQLAlchemy connections within five, and sessions within 128. Container memory stays below 90% of its limit, with last-quarter median growth over the first-quarter median no greater than 32 MiB. Raw Docker memory samples must be present in the final run.

The first sustained run remains failed because it dropped 316 reports and exceeded the end-to-end latency budget. The second run remains failed under its earlier zero-timer-warning rule, despite accepting all 1,800,000 reports and delivering p95 at 131 ms. It had 126 reports over 100 ms late, with scheduling p99 of 2 ms and maximum of 150 ms. An independent architecture reviewer identified the zero-warning rule as an unjustified hard timing constraint on an ordinary operating system and required a sustained-rate check when replacing it. Both earlier raw results are preserved; they are not rescored as passed. Only the fresh run establishes acceptance under this policy.
