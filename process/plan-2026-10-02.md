# Fleetline 100k rewrite: Kafka ingest, NATS fanout

Fleetline sustains 100,000 devices reporting every 5 s (20,000 reports/s) on this laptop, with the same reconciliation discipline as the 10k campaign. Its architecture scales to 1M by adding partitions and processes; 1M is stated as a sizing calculation from measured per-core numbers, never claimed as run. Follows the review [audit-2026-10-02](audit-2026-10-02.md); supersedes the single-process design of [plan-2026-10-01](plan-2026-10-01.md).

## Why the current design stops at ~4k/s

Measured on the 2k/s baseline (`evidence/baseline.json`): the app process runs at a median 48% of one core, so one Python process tops out at about 4k/s. The single sequential processor spends about 9 ms per batch, so at 100 batches/s it has no headroom left. Every browser tab receives the entire position stream. PostgreSQL sits at 10%. The Docker VM has 14 CPUs and 8 GB.

## Approach

- **Ingest.** Stateless FastAPI processes (multi-worker). Devices report over a persistent WebSocket `/ingest`; `POST /locations` and `/locations/batch` stay. Each report is validated and produced to the Kafka topic `reports`, keyed by `device_id`. The response is 202 once Kafka acks. Per-report accepted/stale/duplicate statuses leave the contract.
- **Processors.** A Kafka consumer group: each partition has exactly one owner, and the group rebalances on failure. Each batch reads watermarks, discards duplicates and stale samples, runs PostGIS matching for every fresh sample, does a monotonic upsert (`WHERE reported_at < excluded.reported_at`, which keeps it safe against a zombie owner during rebalance), commits, publishes to NATS, then commits offsets. Delivery is at-least-once; replay is made idempotent by the watermarks.
- **Fanout.** NATS core. Positions go to `fleet.<tile>`; alerts to `alerts.<user>`; zone changes to `zones.<user>`. WS gateways subscribe, with refcounts, only to the tiles in their clients' viewports and the users connected to them. Each connection gets its viewport, conflated to the latest position per device, flushed in compact frames. Alerts are never conflated. The bounded per-connection queues and eviction stay.
- **Kafka over Redis Streams:** Kafka ships partition ownership and rebalancing; on Redis that would be hand-rolled. **NATS over Kafka for fanout:** NATS routes server-side by subject (interest-based); Kafka would make every gateway decode the full stream.
- **Spatial.** One generated `footprint geometry` column: a conservative lon/lat box (MultiPolygon when it crosses the antimeridian, full longitude range near the poles) built by an IMMUTABLE SQL function. GiST candidate filter on it, then the exact `ST_DWithin` on geography. This replaces the radius buckets and btree_gist.
- **Overlap bug.** Delete `max_matches` and `output_bytes` from processing. Bound load with a per-user zone quota at CRUD and the per-connection queues.
- **Generator.** Multiprocess; each process owns a slice of devices, reporting on an open-loop schedule over multiplexed WebSockets. Devices spread realistically across a region, not a 1 km square.
- **Acceptance.** 100k devices, 900 s. Every scheduled report is acked and committed, with consumer lag 0 at the end. Owner sessions reconcile every alert by checksum; the other user gets none. The viewport session sees every device in view, and its final positions match the DB. Delivery p95 < 1 s from the scheduled timestamp. Memory is stable. Scaling sweep: 25k / 50k / 100k.
